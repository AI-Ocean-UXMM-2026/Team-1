# Oern's design holds up, but Burmese text and cash flows need work

Oern's design system is broadly sound against the research: a navy structure, one dark orange action, blue for live things, 48px targets, bottom-anchored primaries and a separate COD signal all match documented practice. The research and the code check show seven issues, and they are not equally well evidenced. The strongest is **Burmese typography**, where Oern's Latin-tuned 1.33-1.43 line-heights sit below the roughly 1.6 an informal test found necessary for tall scripts ([SuttaCentral](https://discourse.suttacentral.net/t/testing-for-line-height-with-tall-scripts/6196)), and where fixed-height chips and rows will likely clip. Second is the **colour system's reliance on hue**: the green, amber and red states collapse for red-green colour-blind users (researchers' computation), and one text pair fails WCAG AA at 4.34:1. Third is **cash and evidence handling for COD**: the rider flow has no proof of delivery beyond a status change, no offline queue for exception writes, and no mismatch or reconciliation path, yet the market is cash-heavy. Fourth is **drift between DESIGN.md and the code**, which in places is large (button radius, focus ring, disabled style). Fifth is a **4.34:1 contrast failure** on secondary text over the subtle fill. The fixes are mostly cheap token or CSS changes. The weakest part of the evidence is Myanmar-specific: almost no competitor UI, address convention or colour-meaning source could be retrieved, so several recommendations rest on inference and need local validation.

How to read the evidence: the underlying notes separate four kinds of claim, and this report keeps them apart. **Documented** means a fetched standard, vendor page or study (cited inline). **Computed** means the researchers' own arithmetic (WCAG ratios, APCA, colour-blindness simulation using Machado 2009 matrices, pixel-to-millimetre conversion), which is reproducible but not independently verified. **Inferred** means reasoning from documented facts. **Unsourced** background claims in the notes (Burmese digit usage, formal register, change-making pain) are left out here. Many primary pages (Grab, Uber Base, Material 3, Apple HIG, Gojek, Polaris, NN/g) failed to load, so competitor colour and control claims are thin and often come from third-party aggregators.

## Colour: the palette is defensible, but hue carries too much meaning

**What the competitors tell us is limited.** Verifiable colour documentation for delivery apps is scarce. Grab specifies its green by Pantone 355C, not hex ([Grab brand centre](https://merchant.grab.com/en-my/brand-centre/grab)); other brand hexes (Gojek, Lalamove, DoorDash, Deliveroo) come from aggregators that admit estimates and are low confidence. The most useful documented pattern is Lalamove's driver app, which uses red-orange for the current order, orange for later same-day and blue for next-day-or-later ([Lalamove](https://www.lalamove.com/en-sg/blog/driving-with-lalamove-what-do-all-those-colours-mean-and-when-do-i-actually-pick-up-the-orders)). That shows an orange family signalling urgency in a driver app, which sits close to Oern's rule that orange is action, never status. Maps follow a different convention: Google uses blue or gray for your route and green/orange/red for traffic ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/3092439?hl=en)), so Oern's blue route with a white casing is the mainstream choice. The researchers infer that pairing a brand orange that fails text contrast with a darker action orange is a common pattern, but that is not documented for these apps.

**Contrast (computed against documented WCAG thresholds).** WCAG 1.4.3 requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for UI components and graphical objects ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum); [W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)). Against these, nearly every Oern text pair passes: white on Kiln Orange `#c2410c` is **5.18:1**, white on the pressed `#9a3412` is 7.31:1, and navy on canvas is 13.48:1. Two things do not pass. Slate `#64748b` on the subtle fill `#f1f5f9` is **4.34:1**, a failure for text but fine for borders and icons. Signal Orange `#f97316` is 2.80:1 with white, which DESIGN.md already forbids. APCA, the draft WCAG 3 candidate, is stricter: its guidance puts body text at Lc 90 preferred and Lc 75 minimum for 18px/400 or 16px/500 ([APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell)). White on Kiln scores Lc -80, and 14px/400 pairs fall around Lc 67-78. Because APCA is still a draft, treat it as a tiebreaker, not a pass/fail gate. Also note the green `#16a34a` and amber `#d97706` solid colours are only 3.30 and 3.19 on white: acceptable as icons, not as text.

**Outdoor use raises the bar.** Ambient light adds reflected luminance to both light and dark states of a display, so indoor ratios overstate real contrast ([USPTO application](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12397623)). One 2024 display paper cites an ambient contrast above 5:1 as a useful target (via search summary only; full text unavailable) ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/bd8af37406d344db817bc71d5cd1f2cc)). The note also found a thesis claiming 18:1 is needed in high illumination, but this was seen only through a snippet and is low confidence. No study on pastel fills or font weight in sunlight was found. The researchers' inference, which this report adopts with moderate confidence, is that pale washes (about 1.1:1 against white) wash out under glare, so meaning must sit in text, icon and border, and critical text (COD amount, primary buttons) should aim for 7:1 or better.

**Colour-blindness is the sharpest colour risk (computed).** About 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have colour vision deficiency, mostly red-green ([Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/)), and WCAG 1.4.1 forbids colour as the only means of conveying information ([W3C 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color)). The researchers' simulation shows Oern's delivered-green text versus failed-red text has a CIELAB distance of 97 normally but only **8.5 under protanopia**; amber text versus red text is 11 (protan) and 5 (deutan); and green and amber have almost identical luminance (Y 0.269 vs 0.280), so there is no lightness cue. Kiln action orange versus failed red is only 5 under deutan. DESIGN.md already requires a text label on every status and an icon "where space allows", which is the right defence; the finding is that the icon should be mandatory, not optional, and that red text should be made visibly darker than amber text.

**Orange in Myanmar.** The evidence is thin. Burmese monks mostly wear maroon rather than the saffron of Thailand or Sri Lanka ([exotravel](https://www.exotravel.com/blog/en/buddhist-monk/), a weak source), and saffron robes are tied to the 1988 and 2007 protests ([CS Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/Commentary/the-monitors-view/2021/0217/The-saffron-color-of-power-in-Myanmar)). The Myanmar flag's yellow, green and red are tied to unity, peace and bravery ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Myanmar)). The notes found no source on how Burmese users read orange, green or red as UI status. Whether orange is culturally safe for a delivery action is therefore an open question for user testing, not a finding.

## Controls: sizes are right, but the code does not match the spec

**Target size.** WCAG 2.5.8 requires only 24x24 CSS px ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum)); Material and web.dev recommend 48 px with about 8 px spacing ([Android accessibility help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858); [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/accessible-tap-targets)). Empirical studies give physical sizes: 9.2 mm for discrete one-thumb tasks and 9.6 mm for serial tasks ([Parhi, Karlson and Bederson, 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150)), and 7 mm at screen centre to about 12 mm at corners ([Hoober, UXmatters 2017](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php)). Walking raised selection time 31% and errors 23%, and enlarging targets by 40% almost fully offset that ([Schildbach and Rukzio, 2010](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui.inst.100/institut/Papers/Prof_Rukzio/2010/SchildbachMobileHCI2010.pdf), snippet summary). The researchers computed that on a 360px-wide phone 48 px is only about **8.5-9 mm**, slightly under the Parhi figure, while 56 px is about 10-10.5 mm. The most common Myanmar viewport is 360x800 at 15.2% ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/screen-resolution-stats/mobile/myanmar)). So Oern's 48 px floor is a floor, not comfort, and rider-critical actions (arrived, delivered, COD collected) justify 56-64 px. No study of gloves, wet fingers or motorbike vibration was found; the walking study is the best proxy.

**Hierarchy and shape.** GOV.UK's guidance of one primary per page, sentence-case verb labels, and sparing use of disabled buttons ([GOV.UK Button](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)) matches Oern's One Orange Rule. No evidence was found that pill versus rounded rectangle affects usability, so shape is a brand choice.

**Code versus DESIGN.md (checked directly in `src/app/globals.css` and `src/components/delivery/mui-provider.tsx`).** DESIGN.md says primary phone buttons are full pills at 56px. In the code, the base `.button` is 43px tall with a 10px radius and 12px type; a later override raises it to 52px, **`--radius-md` (12px), 15px / weight 600**, and `.button-small` to 48px; the global MUI Button override sets `minHeight: 48` and `borderRadius: 999`. So class-based buttons are 12px rounded rectangles at 52px, not 56px pills, and MUI buttons are 48px pills; which one a screen gets depends on the markup. `.icon-button` is 48px with a 12px radius, whereas DESIGN.md specifies a pill icon button. DESIGN.md says disabled buttons are 55% opacity; the later override sets `opacity: 1` with a grey fill. DESIGN.md says the focus ring is Focus Apricot `#fdba74` (computed 1.69:1 on white, which fails 1.4.11 as a sole focus indicator). **Both rings ship, depending on the control.** A later global rule in `globals.css` (around line 3763) gives native `button`, `a`, `input`, `select`, `textarea` and `summary` a 3px Live Blue ring (`--color-info`, 5.17:1 on white), which overrides the earlier apricot rule at line 463. But `mui-provider.tsx` (line 46) sets `.Mui-focusVisible` on every MUI ButtonBase to the apricot `#fdba74`, so MUI buttons, chips and list buttons, which the customer app uses heavily, keep the failing ring. (A correction: an earlier draft of this report called the apricot ring a stale documentation entry; the MUI override shows it is live.) This also conflicts with DESIGN.md's rule that Live Blue never marks non-live emphasis.

**Irreversible actions.** WCAG 2.5.1 and 2.5.7 require a single-pointer, non-path alternative to swipe and drag ([W3C 2.5.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html); [W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)), which DESIGN.md already honours ("every swipe has a visible button"). Uber and delivery apps use swipe controls for arrive and start ([Uber](https://www.uber.com/nz/en/drive/basics/how-to-take-trips/)), and Lalamove reportedly uses slide-to-confirm arrival plus a photo at pickup (medium confidence; the claim came from a search summary of another Lalamove page). The researchers' inference, which the evidence supports only as expert consensus, is that confirmation dialogs lose effect when overused and undo suits reversible steps; money is the real irreversible risk, so a COD confirm sheet showing the amount on the button is better than a swipe. Experts also advise keeping submit buttons enabled with an explanation instead of greying them out, and guarding against double submits on both client and server ([Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2024/02/dont-disable-form-controls.html); [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)). An idempotency key per action on flaky networks is the researchers' engineering inference, not a sourced finding.

**Haptics.** `navigator.vibrate` works in Chromium-family Android browsers but never in iOS Safari ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/vibration)), so haptics can only be a progressive enhancement. No study shows haptics help riders; helmets and engine vibration plausibly mask them (speculation).

## Flows: Oern's skeleton fits the norms; the gaps are evidence, recovery and cash

**Customer tracking.** Baymard lists six key tracking elements, led by the expected delivery date, then a stage progress bar, carrier, tracking number, detailed history and contents; only 33% of tested sites provided all six and 25% failed to show an expected date ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/integrate-tracking-info)). Baymard also found wording like "Delivery on or before" led a user to think a delivered parcel was still in transit, and that sending users to a third-party carrier page reads as a glitch. Oern's Track screen (map plus bottom summary with status, ETA, freshness, call and help) fits the live-phase pattern, and its explicit stale-GPS and "never invent a location or ETA" rules are stricter than anything the researchers could verify at competitors. The inference is to make the ETA window and status word the largest element, with a compact stage bar and collapsed history. Two gaps: tracking requires sign-in (PRODUCT.md), so a non-account recipient cannot follow a parcel, whereas Lalamove reportedly offers a share button ([EasyParcel on Lalamove](https://blog.easyparcel.com/my/lalamove-tracking/), third-party); and failure states should offer several recovery options, as Royal Mail does with five redelivery choices ([Royal Mail](https://royalmail.com/redelivery)). The notes found no source that treats forced sign-in as an antipattern, so that is a product trade-off, not a research finding.

**Booking and addresses.** Lalamove's documented order is pickup and drop-off, vehicle and add-ons, notes, payment, review, with the price shown before placing and subject to change ([Lalamove UAE](https://www.lalamove.com/en-ae/blog/how-to-place-order-lalamove-uae)). Oern's three-step flow with a persisted draft is within norms but DESIGN.md does not say a price estimate is shown. Baymard finds automatic address lookup is the most effective way to cut address burden, 9% of manually typed addresses contain typos, and inline validation should fire on blur, not on first keystroke ([Baymard address lookup](https://baymard.com/research-articles/automatic-address-lookup); [Baymard inline validation](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). Postal autodetection is irrelevant in Yangon, where addresses use township and ward ([GeoPostcodes](https://www.geopostcodes.com/country/myanmar/address-validation/)) and many central streets carry both old and new names ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_name_changes_in_Yangon)). The inference is township-first selection, free-text landmark, and a mandatory pin; no Yangon-specific address study was found.

**Manager dispatch.** Onfleet documents a useful model: unassigned tasks pinned to the top of the sidebar and grey on the map, a single delay rule (past the deadline, or an ETA beyond it), and eligibility rules for auto-assignment such as active drivers only and a per-driver task cap ([Onfleet task status](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/20509786766228-Task-Status); [Onfleet driver status](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/10228905705876-Driver-Status)). Detrack's COD search reports expected, collected and handed-over totals per driver with shortage or excess at each stage ([Detrack](https://detrack.com/tutorials/how-to-use-the-cod-search/), search summary). The inference: show why a rider is recommended or excluded, use one delay definition across customer and manager screens, and add a per-rider daily COD reconciliation view. On phones, the manager's priority should be Needs attention, the unassigned queue and the assign action, not the map; no source documents dispatcher mobile behaviour.

**Rider flow.** From the repo, Oern has a lockable job board, a distance-guarded "I've arrived", a separate COD chip and a reason-based failure flow. A grep (not a full read) found no photo, signature, PIN or accept-timer handling, and the can't-deliver submit shows "You're offline. This needs a connection to record." Competitors document photo or barcode proof ([Onfleet](https://onfleet.com/proof-of-delivery)), PIN or signature as API options ([Uber developer docs](https://developer.uber.com/docs/deliveries/guides/proof-of-delivery)), and required-signature reasons on failed deliveries ([Detrack](https://help.detrack.com/en/articles/6553420-how-to-capture-a-signature-for-specific-failed-delivery-collection-reasons-in-the-driver-app)). A photo alone is weak: a news anecdote describes a driver photographing an order on a doorstep and then taking it ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/uber-eats-driver-customer-takeaway-blackpool-163332288.html)). On COD, DoorDash lets a driver report a collection issue with a reason, keeping base pay ([DoorDash](https://help.doordash.com/en-au/dashers/article/cash-on-delivery-overview-and-faq)), and Lalamove labels wallet-paid orders so drivers never ask for cash ([Lalamove](https://www.lalamove.com/en-ae/blog/how-to-check-payment-mode-is-it-a-cash-or-wallet-payment)). For failed deliveries, DoorDash requires a call and text first and a wait timer before a guided safe-drop ([DoorDash](https://help.doordash.com/en-us/dashers/article/how-to-complete-a-delivery-when-the-customer-is-unavailable)), while SPX customer reviews complain of riders marking parcels failed without contact ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/spx.ph), anecdotal). The notes found no precedent for short- or over-payment UI, so that design is the researchers' judgement. Accept timers carry penalties at Grab and DoorDash ([Grab](https://www.grab.com/my/ms/blog/driver/car/driver-timeout-feature-protecting-high-performing-driver-partners/); [DoorDash](https://help.doordash.com/en-us/dashers/article/time-earnings-mode)); since Oern assigns jobs, timers are not recommended.

## Myanmar conditions: Burmese text is the biggest concrete risk

**Burmese typography.** Oern self-hosts Noto Sans Myanmar at 400/600/700 after Inter, which is the right architecture. But `<html lang="en">` is set, no `:lang(my)` rule exists, and the code has many hard-coded line-heights, some `line-height: 1` or `1.08` on `overflow: hidden` elements (verified by the note's grep of `globals.css`, line numbers cited there). DESIGN.md's 14px/20px body (1.43) and 12px/16px label (1.33) are below the roughly 1.6 an informal test recommended after finding clipping at 1.1 ([SuttaCentral](https://discourse.suttacentral.net/t/testing-for-line-height-with-tall-scripts/6196)); a real bug shows Burmese UI text cut off at the top in Tor Browser with the same Noto font ([Tor issue 41051](https://gitlab.torproject.org/tpo/applications/tor-browser/-/issues/41051)). The notes' inferences, with moderate confidence: use Burmese-specific tokens of about 1.6-1.7 line-height (for example 14/24 body, 12/20 label), switch fixed 28px chips, 64px rows and 56px buttons to `min-height`, set `lang="my"` on Burmese text, and map Oern's 500/650/750 weights to the shipped 400/600/700 deliberately, since browsers will otherwise pick the nearest face. A 13-14px floor for Burmese labels is also an inference, with no formal minimum found.

**Zawgyi and input.** Zawgyi dominated before the 2019 switch (Bagan Keyboard data reported roughly 85% Zawgyi) ([Burma Library](https://www.burmalibrary.org/en/myanmar-switch-to-unicode-to-take-two-years-app-developer)), and Facebook built automatic converters ([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/android/unicode-font-converter/)). No 2024-2026 share was found, so how much Zawgyi still appears in rider- and customer-typed addresses and notes is unknown. Google's Myanmar Tools and the ICU `Zawgyi-my` transliterator exist ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawgyi_font)), which makes detect-and-convert on write a cheap safeguard.

**Bandwidth, power and devices.** Ookla's median mobile download was reported at 5.09 Mbps in early 2025, down 78% year on year, from a single secondary report ([DataReportal 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-myanmar)). There have been 300+ shutdowns since 2021, a VPN ban, tower damage and long power cuts ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/myanmar-internet-blackouts-app-vpn-bans-starlink/)); Yangon rotation was reported at 4 hours on and 8 off in January 2025 ([Myanmar Now](https://myanmar-now.org/en/news/myanmar-suffers-worsening-power-cuts-as-junta-prioritizes-military-zones/)). Android 11 to 16 each hold roughly 8-19% of traffic and Xiaomi leads vendors, though StatCounter measures web traffic, not installed base ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/android-version-market-share/mobile/myanmar)). The notes' engineering inferences, unsourced as numbers, are a critical payload under roughly 150-200 KB, an offline queue for rider writes, and a low-data map mode. A specific risk: PRODUCT.md says rider GPS works only while the web app is open, so Oern depends on foreground tracking that aggressive phone battery managers may break (OEM battery behaviour is unsourced background knowledge).

**Cash and competitors.** A 2020 study said most Facebook purchases were paid cash on delivery and "cash remains king" ([ISEAS Fulcrum](https://fulcrum.sg/the-advent-of-cashless-transactions-in-myanmar-cash-is-still-king/)); a 2026 vendor guide gives COD around 35% and wallets 40% but is low confidence ([EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/myanmar-ecommerce-cod-shopify-market-guide-2026)). COD therefore stays central, but its current share is unverified. The only confirmed local competitor is foodpanda Myanmar, operating since December 2019 ([BNI Online](https://www.bnionline.net/en/node/91390)); no UI documentation was obtained for Wave, KBZPay, Ninja Van, Royal Express or others, so claims about local UI conventions (bilingual toggle, currency format, 12-hour time) are untested and omitted.

**Safety and privacy.** The Cybersecurity Law took effect on 30 July 2025, reportedly with three-year data retention, and sources conflict on whether VPN users or only providers are penalised ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/mm/publication/articles/31053/cybersecurity-law-in-force-30-july-2025); [RFA](https://rfa.org/english/myanmar/2025/01/02/cybersecurity-law-vpn/)). The researchers infer that Oern's rule of tracking riders only from pickup to delivery is sound, and that breadcrumbs and address-phone links should be minimised and shortened; whether Mapbox or Supabase endpoints are reachable in Myanmar was not verified.

## What Oern already does right

Oern already follows much of what the research supports. It separates delivery state from COD payment state as two signals, which no retrieved competitor documents as clearly. It reserves blue for live data, matching map convention, and uses a white-cased route. It requires text labels on statuses, bans `#94a3b8` for text, and uses a single dark orange for action with a verified 5.18:1 white-on-fill ratio. It keeps targets at 48 px with 8 px spacing, anchors primaries in the bottom third, and requires a button alternative to every swipe, which satisfies WCAG 2.5.1 and 2.5.7. It self-hosts Burmese and Latin fonts (good for unreliable networks), uses tabular figures, and shows "last updated" and stale-GPS states. It persists the pickup draft, attaches help to a parcel, uses a distance-conditional "arrived" guard instead of friction on every stop, has a reason-based failed-delivery flow with a next action, and collects rider location only after pickup. It uses light theme only, which avoids a dark-mode palette that sunlight does not require.

## Open questions and what to verify next

Several claims need checking before action. First, **which style wins per screen**: audit which `.button` and MUI cascade each screen receives, and what MUI's focus style does, since the notes only spot-checked. Second, the claim that **no POD, photo, PIN or accept-timer code exists** comes from a grep of the rider files; confirm by reading them and the COD tables. Third, **Burmese rendering**: test real Burmese strings at 12-16 px on low-end Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo phones, and inspect the font files' coverage and weights, since none of this was done. Fourth, **colour meaning in Myanmar**: run a short test with Burmese users on how they read orange, green, amber and red status, since no source exists. Fifth, **outdoor legibility**: test the app on real devices in Yangon sunlight, since the evidence (including the 18:1 figure) is low confidence and no weight or pastel study was found. Sixth, **competitor teardown**: install Wave, KBZPay, foodpanda Myanmar and any local courier apps and document their status colours, language toggles, currency formats and COD flows, because none of these could be fetched. Seventh, **COD share and rider cash remittance norms** are unverified; interview a manager. Eighth, check **SMS reachability** before choosing PIN or OTP for delivery proof, and whether **Mapbox and Supabase** hosts are reachable under Myanmar's restrictions. Ninth, confirm the Burmese digit and register conventions, which the notes left unsourced.

## Conclusion

The research does not argue for a new design system; it argues for closing the gap between a Latin-first spec and a Burmese-first, cash-first, sun-and-motorbike context. The surprise is that the highest-value changes are small and mostly mechanical: Burmese line-height tokens, an icon requirement on status chips, one text-colour swap, and a DESIGN.md that finally matches the stylesheet. The larger investments, offline-safe rider writes and a COD reconciliation and evidence loop, are justified by Myanmar's network, power and cash realities more than by any competitor precedent.

The thinness of the evidence is itself a finding. Almost nothing about Myanmar UI conventions, local competitors, address entry, or colour meaning could be documented, and several of the most useful comparisons rest on vendor help pages, aggregators or the researchers' own computation. Oern's best next step is a small round of in-country testing (Burmese rendering, sunlight legibility, and status-colour reading), which would convert most of the medium-confidence recommendations above into decisions.
